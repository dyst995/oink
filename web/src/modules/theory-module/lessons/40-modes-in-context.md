# Modes in context

**Prerequisites:** Functional harmony; major/minor fluency  
**Next:** Arpeggios and chord-tone targeting

## Concept

A **mode** is a scale type obtained by treating a different degree of a parent scale as the tonic. The seven modes of the major scale:

| Mode | Starts on degree | Formula | Characteristic degree |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ionian | 1 | `1 2 3 4 5 6 7` | the major scale itself |
| Dorian | 2 | `1 2 ♭3 4 5 6 ♭7` | natural `6` against `♭3` |
| Phrygian | 3 | `1 ♭2 ♭3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7` | `♭2` |
| Lydian | 4 | `1 2 3 ♯4 5 6 7` | `♯4` |
| Mixolydian | 5 | `1 2 3 4 5 6 ♭7` | `♭7` against `3` |
| Aeolian | 6 | `1 2 ♭3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7` | natural minor |
| Locrian | 7 | `1 ♭2 ♭3 4 ♭5 ♭6 ♭7` | `♭5` |

Ionian is the major scale and Aeolian is natural minor, so five modes are new. A mode exists only when its tonic is **heard** as the tonic. The same seven notes with a different tonic are a different sound.

## Underlying logic

Two ways to build a mode:

- **From the parent:** `D` Dorian uses the notes of `C` major, starting and ending on `D`: `D E F G A B C`. This is fast, but it gives only the notes, not the sound.
- **From the formula:** `D` Dorian is `D` natural minor with the sixth raised: `D E F G A B C`. Comparing against the parallel major or minor shows what makes the mode distinct.

Differences from the parallel scale on the same root:

- Dorian: natural minor with a raised sixth (natural `6`).
- Phrygian: natural minor with a lowered second (`♭2`).
- Lydian: major with a raised fourth (`♯4`).
- Mixolydian: major with a lowered seventh (`♭7`).
- Locrian: natural minor with a lowered second and fifth.

Modes with a major third are Ionian, Lydian, and Mixolydian. Those with a minor third are Dorian, Phrygian, Aeolian, and Locrian. Locrian has `♭5`, so its tonic triad `1 ♭3 ♭5` is diminished and cannot be a stable home. That is why Locrian is rare as a tonic mode.

**Modal versus functional.** In **functional** harmony, V (or V7) pulls to I through the leading tone, and a cadence establishes the key. In **modal** harmony, the tonic is established by repetition, a drone, or a vamp, and by stressing the mode's characteristic degree. Functional resolution is weak or absent.

The characteristic degree must be in a chord or a prominent melody note to be heard. Playing the parent scale over a chord does not by itself make a mode.

## Musical significance

Modes are used for specific colors, not as seven interchangeable scales:

- Dorian: minor with a lift. Vamp `Dm – G` (`i – IV`). The major `IV` chord contains the natural `B` that defines Dorian.
- Phrygian: dark, Spanish-flavored. Vamp `Em – F` (`i – ♭II`). The `F` chord contains `F`, the `♭2`.
- Lydian: bright and floating. Vamp `F – G` (`I – II`). The `G` chord contains `B`, the `♯4` of `F`.
- Mixolydian: major with a relaxed, bluesy lean. Vamp `G – F` (`I – ♭VII`). The `F` chord contains `F`, the `♭7` of `G`.
- Locrian: unstable, usually brief.

Many tunes sound modal because the chords avoid a strong V to I. `G – F – G` is likely Mixolydian: the `F` chord is the cue.

## Guitar application

Offsets in half steps above the root, valid on any one string:

- Dorian: `0 2 3 5 7 9 10 12`
- Phrygian: `0 1 3 5 7 8 10 12`
- Lydian: `0 2 4 6 7 9 11 12`
- Mixolydian: `0 2 4 5 7 9 10 12`

Examples:

- `D` Dorian on string 4 (open `D`): frets `0 2 3 5 7 9 10 12` give `D E F G A B C D`. Against `D` natural minor (`0 2 3 5 7 8 10 12`), only fret 9 versus 8 differs: `B` versus `B♭`.
- `E` Phrygian on string 6: frets `0 1 3 5 7 8 10 12` give `E F G A B C D E`. Against `E` natural minor (`0 2 3 5 7 8 10 12`), only the second is lowered: `F♯` becomes `F`.
- `F` Lydian on string 6 from fret 1: frets `1 3 5 7 8 10 12 13` give `F G A B C D E F`. Against `F` major (`1 3 5 6 8 10 12 13`), the fourth rises from `B♭` (fret 6) to `B` (fret 7).
- `G` Mixolydian on string 3 (open `G`): frets `0 2 4 5 7 9 10 12` give `G A B C D E F G`. Against `G` major (`0 2 4 5 7 9 11 12`), fret 10 replaces fret 11: `F` for `F♯`.

Parent finder: the mode starting on degree `n` of a major scale. `A` Mixolydian: `A` is degree 5 of `D` major, so the notes are `D E F♯ G A B C♯` read from `A`: `A B C♯ D E F♯ G`.

To hear a mode, play the vamp chords and sit on the tonic. For Dorian: loop `Dm – G`, then play `D E F G A B C D` up and down, landing on `D` and on `B` over the `G`.

## Exercises

1. Write all seven modes of `C` major with their starting notes. Expected: `C D E F G A B`, `D E F G A B C`, `E F G A B C D`, `F G A B C D E`, `G A B C D E F`, `A B C D E F G`, `B C D E F G A`.
2. Spell `A` Dorian, `B` Phrygian, `C` Lydian, `D` Mixolydian. Expected: `A B C D E F♯ G`; `B C D E F♯ G A`; `C D E F♯ G A B`; `D E F♯ G A B C`.
3. Name the parent major scale for `E` Dorian, `A` Mixolydian, `F♯` Phrygian, `B♭` Lydian. Expected: `D`, `D`, `D`, `F`.
4. Spell `G` Dorian by formula (`1 2 ♭3 4 5 6 ♭7`) and check that it equals `F` major read from `G`. Expected: `G A B♭ C D E F`.
5. On string 6, play `E` natural minor, then `E` Phrygian. Name the one fret that moves. Expected: fret 2 becomes fret 1.
6. On string 3, play `G` major, then `G` Mixolydian. Name the changed note. Expected: `F♯` (fret 11) becomes `F` (fret 10).
7. Play the vamp `Dm – G` for eight bars and improvise using only `D` Dorian notes, ending on `D`. Listen for the `B` over `G`.
8. Play the vamp `G – F` and improvise `G` Mixolydian. Then change the second chord to `D` major (`G – D`). The `F♯` in `D` pulls up to `G` like a leading tone, so the sound becomes functional, `I – V` in `G` major. Describe how the sense of home and the role of `F` versus `F♯` changed.

## Mastery criteria

- **Conceptual:** Distinguish a mode from a scale played from another note. Explain how a tonic is established and why the characteristic degree matters.
- **Written:** Spell any mode of `C G D F` major by parent and by formula, and state its characteristic degree.
- **Fretboard:** Build Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, and Mixolydian on one string by offsets. Show how each differs from the parallel major or minor.
- **Aural:** Identify the characteristic degree against the root by ear in at least Dorian, Phrygian, and Mixolydian.
- **Application:** For a vamp, name the likely mode and justify it with a chord containing the characteristic degree.
