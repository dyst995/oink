# Minor-key harmony

**Prerequisites:** Natural/harmonic minor; diatonic triads  
**Next:** Seventh chords

## Concept

Minor keys use **more than one scale resource** for harmony. Natural minor supplies a diatonic chord set; **harmonic minor** (raised 7) creates a major `V` / `V7` and a leading tone; **melodic minor** (raised 6 and 7) appears in melodic lines and some chord colors.

Natural minor diatonic triads (example `A` minor):

| Degree | RN | Chord in `A` minor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | i | `Am` |
| 2 | ii° | `B°` |
| 3 | ♭III | `C` |
| 4 | iv | `Dm` |
| 5 | v | `Em` |
| 6 | ♭VI | `F` |
| 7 | ♭VII | `G` |

With **harmonic minor** (raise `G` → `G♯` in A minor): `v` becomes **`V`** (`E`) or **`V7`** (`E7`), and `vii°` becomes sharper leading-tone material (`G♯°`). That dominant is why `E7 → Am` feels conclusive.

## Underlying logic

Natural minor’s ♭7 makes `v` minor — weak pull to `i`. Raising 7 restores the leading-tone relationship familiar from major. Raised 6 (melodic) smooths melodic motion into the leading tone and can yield major `IV` color in minor contexts.

Roman numerals in minor must state whether you assume natural or include harmonic-minor chords (`V` vs `v`).

## Musical significance

Most minor songs mix these resources: `i–iv–V7–i`, `i–♭VII–♭VI–V`, Aeolian rock loops with ♭VII, classical minor with raised leading tones. Analysis asks which 6 and 7 are in play.

## Guitar application

In `A` minor:

- Play `| Am | Dm | Em | Am |` (natural `v`)
- Replace with `| Am | Dm | E7 | Am |` (harmonic `V7`) — hear the pull of `G♯`
- Voice-lead `E7` shells into `Am` on the neck; resolve `G♯→A` and `D→C`

Cadences: authentic in minor is often `V7→i`; Aeolian closes may use `♭VII→i` with different weight.

## Exercises

1. Write natural-minor diatonic triads for `E` minor and `D` minor with RN.
2. In `A` minor, spell `V7` from harmonic minor and show the leading tone resolving to tonic in note names.
3. Analyze `| Am | F | G | E7 | Am |` with RN; mark which chord needs raised 7.
4. On guitar, play `i–iv–V7–i` and `i–iv–v–i` in `Am`; describe the cadential difference.
5. Build a 4-bar minor progression that uses ♭VII and a final `V7–i`.

## Mastery criteria

- **Written:** Produce natural-minor diatonic triads and harmonic-minor `V`/`V7` in any minor key.
- **Conceptual:** Explain why raised 7 creates dominant function in minor.
- **Practical:** Play and hear `v` vs `V7` into `i` on guitar.
- **Application:** Label a minor progression’s RN including borrowed/raised-scale chords when present.
