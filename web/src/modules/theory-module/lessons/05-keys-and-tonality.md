# Keys and tonality

**Prerequisites:** Constructing the major scale  
**Next:** Scale degrees and formulas

## Concept

A **key** is a pitch collection organized around a home pitch class called the **tonic**. “In the key of G major” means: the major-scale recipe built from `G`, with `G` as the center of gravity.

The recipe does not change when the key changes. Only the **starting pitch** changes. `C` major and `E` major are the same pattern of whole and half steps, locked to different tonics.

**Tonality** here means music that treats one pitch class as home. Melodies and chords feel resolved when they return to that tonic (and to chords built on it — later). The key tells you which seven pitch classes are the default “inside” notes.

Spelling follows the key so each degree keeps a unique letter. Sharp keys accumulate sharps in a fixed order (`F♯`, then `C♯`, then `G♯`, …). Flat keys accumulate flats (`B♭`, then `E♭`, then `A♭`, …). You do not need to memorize the full circle yet; build each key with WWHWWWH and the letter rule, and the accidentals will match that order.

“In the key of X” does **not** mean every note of a solo must be from that scale. It means the piece’s home collection and tonic are X. Chromatic notes can appear; they are heard against that home.

## Examples

- Key of `C` major: pitch classes `C D E F G A B`, tonic `C`. No sharps or flats.
- Key of `G` major: `G A B C D E F♯`, tonic `G`. One sharp: `F♯`.
- Key of `F` major: `F G A B♭ C D E`, tonic `F`. One flat: `B♭`.
- Same tune moved from `C` to `D` major: same step relationships, every pitch class shifted so the tonic is `D`.

## Exercises

1. Build and write the major collections for `A`, `E♭`, and `E`. Circle the tonic in each.
2. For each collection above, list the accidentals only. Confirm they are all sharps or all flats (not mixed).
3. Take a short major melody you know in `C` (even three notes). Rewrite it in `G` by keeping the same scale degrees relative to the new tonic (or sing it starting on `G` and name the pitch classes).

**Done when:** you can say what “key of X major” means in one sentence, and you can build that key’s seven pitch classes from the tonic without copying a chart.
