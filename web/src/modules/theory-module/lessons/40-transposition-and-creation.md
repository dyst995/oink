# Transposition and creation

**Prerequisites:** All previous lessons, especially diatonic harmony, functional progressions, voice leading, and analyzing real music  
**Next:** Curriculum complete; keep refining

## Concept

**Transposition** is moving music to a different pitch level while keeping its internal relationships: the same intervals, the same scale degrees, the same Roman numerals. **Creation** is using what you know deliberately to make new music: choosing a key, a form, harmony, voice leading, a melody, and a feel, and then revising them.

These two skills belong together. Transposition tests whether you understand structure rather than just fingerings. Creation gives your knowledge somewhere to go.

## Underlying logic

### Transposing by semitones

To transpose up `n` semitones, move every pitch class up `n` semitones. The twelve pitch classes in order are `C C♯ D E♭ E F F♯ G A♭ A B♭ B`. To transpose `C Am F G7` up three semitones: `C` to `E♭`, `A` to `C`, `F` to `A♭`, `G` to `B♭`, giving `E♭ Cm A♭ B♭7`.

### Transposing by Roman numerals

Write the progression as numerals, then rewrite those numerals in the new key. A `I vi IV V` is:

| Key | I | vi | IV | V |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `C` | `C` | `Am` | `F` | `G` |
| `G` | `G` | `Em` | `C` | `D` |
| `D` | `D` | `Bm` | `G` | `A` |
| `A` | `A` | `F♯m` | `D` | `E` |
| `E` | `E` | `C♯m` | `A` | `B` |

This is faster and less error-prone than shifting each chord, and it works for any key you can spell.

### Transposing melodies

Keep the **scale degrees**, not the fret numbers. A melody `E G A G` in `C` major is degrees `3 5 6 5`. In `G` major the same degrees are `B D E D`. If the melody lies on one string set and has no open strings, you can slide the whole line up or down the neck by the same number of frets. If you move it to a different string set, the `G` to `B` tuning gap changes the shape, so check the notes.

### Capo and open shapes

A **capo** at fret `n` raises every open string by `n` semitones. A `G` shape with a capo at fret 2 sounds as `A` major. Open shapes cannot be slid along the neck, because the open strings stay the same pitch; barre shapes and shells can.

### Creating deliberately

A piece is built from decisions. Use this order:

1. **Key and mood.** Choose a key and a scale color (major, natural minor, Mixolydian).
2. **Form.** Choose a length: 8 or 12 bars is enough.
3. **Harmony.** Write a progression with a clear function plan (`T PD D T`) and one deliberate color: a secondary dominant, a borrowed chord, or a substitution.
4. **Voice leading.** Choose voicings so the guide tones move by half step or whole step.
5. **Feel and pulse.** Choose tempo, subdivision, and straight or swung eighths.
6. **Melody.** Write or improvise a motif, develop it, and shape phrases with rests.
7. **Outline the changes.** Check that the melody lands on chord tones at the changes.
8. **Revise.** Play the piece, find the weakest bar, and fix one thing.

### A sample plan

Key: `G` major. Eight bars. Progression: `Gmaj7 | E7 | Am7 | D7 | Bm7 | Cmaj7 | Cm7 | Gmaj7`.

| Bar | Chord | Numeral | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `Gmaj7` | `Imaj7` | home |
| 2 | `E7` | `V7/ii` | secondary dominant aimed at `Am7` |
| 3 | `Am7` | `ii7` | predominant |
| 4 | `D7` | `V7` | dominant |
| 5 | `Bm7` | `iii7` | tonic substitute, softer arrival |
| 6 | `Cmaj7` | `IVmaj7` | predominant |
| 7 | `Cm7` | `iv7` | borrowed from `G` minor, darkens the `IV` |
| 8 | `Gmaj7` | `Imaj7` | home |

## Musical significance

Being able to change key means you can play with other musicians and singers, fit a part to a vocal range, and see that one idea can appear at different pitch levels. Deliberate creation shows you which choices produce which effects. Over time, choices that were deliberate become habits and sound natural.

## Guitar application

### Transposing a progression with shells

Take the `ii V I` shells in `C` (`Dm7 x5x56x`, `G7 3xx46x`, `Cmaj7 x3x45x`). Slide each shell up two frets for `D` major: `Em7 x7x78x`, `A7 5xx68x`, `Dmaj7 x5x67x`. The shape stays the same; the roots move.

### Capo

Play `G Em C D` with a capo at fret 2, using the same shapes. Name the key that sounds (`A` major) and the chords that sound (`A F♯m D E`).

### Creating a short piece

1. Choose a key and write an eight-bar progression with numerals.
2. Choose a voicing for each chord and write the guide tones.
3. Write a motif and place it on bar 1. Develop it by sequence over bars 2 to 4.
4. Record the chord loop with a metronome and a feel you choose.
5. Improvise over it three times, each with a different goal: chord tones only, chord tones plus approach notes, and then free.
6. Keep what works, change one thing, and record again.

## Exercises

1. Transpose `Dm7 G7 Cmaj7` to `F`, `G`, and `E♭`. Answers: `Gm7 C7 Fmaj7`; `Am7 D7 Gmaj7`; `Fm7 B♭7 E♭maj7`.
2. Transpose `E A B A` (a 12-bar blues fragment) from `E` to `A`. Answer: `A D E D`.
3. Write the numerals for `C Am F G7` and rewrite in `A` and in `E♭`. Answers: `A F♯m D E7`; `E♭ Cm A♭ B♭7`.
4. Transpose the melody `E G A G` (in `C` major) to `G` major and to `F` major by degrees. Answers: `B D E D` in `G`; `A C D C` in `F`.
5. Play a 12-bar blues in `A` and then in `E` using the same shapes. State which shapes you changed and which you did not.
6. Complete the creation steps for a piece of at least eight bars. Your result must include: a numeral analysis, at least one non-diatonic chord with an explanation, shell or other voicings with guide tones marked, a motif with one development technique, a stated feel, and a recording. Play it in two keys.

## Mastery criteria

- **Conceptual:** explain transposition as preserving intervals, degrees, and numerals, and explain why the capo and sliding barre shapes do this.
- **Written:** transpose a progression and a melody to a new key by numerals and degrees.
- **Fretboard:** play a progression in two keys with movable voicings and no open-position fallback.
- **Aural:** recognize that the same progression in two keys has the same function.
- **Application:** compose or improvise a piece over a progression you wrote, explaining each choice with a concept from the curriculum, then revise it after listening.
