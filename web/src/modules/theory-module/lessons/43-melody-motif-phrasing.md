# Melody, motif, and phrasing

**Prerequisites:** Intervals; arpeggios; rhythm  
**Next:** Analyzing real music

## Concept

A **motif** is a short musical idea, usually two to six notes, defined by its rhythm and its contour (the pattern of ups and downs). A **phrase** is a longer unit, often two or four bars, that feels like a spoken sentence: it has a start, a direction, and a point where it rests or pauses.

Melody is made of choices about notes, rhythm, and space. **Development** means taking a motif and changing it in recognizable ways so the listener hears one idea growing instead of unrelated notes. **Articulation** is how each note is played (attack, connection, and release). **Tension and release** is the movement between unstable notes or chords and stable ones.

## Underlying logic

### Motif: rhythm plus contour

Two motifs with the same pitches but different rhythm sound like different ideas. Two motifs with the same rhythm and a different contour also sound different. A motif is recognized mainly by rhythm and shape, so keeping the rhythm while changing the pitches is the easiest way to make a variation that still sounds related.

### Ways to develop a motif

| Technique | What changes | What stays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repetition | nothing | everything |
| Sequence | pitch level (moved up or down by scale steps) | rhythm, contour |
| Rhythmic variation | rhythm | pitches |
| Inversion | contour flipped (ups become downs) | rhythm, step sizes |
| Fragmentation | only part of the motif is used | the part's rhythm and pitches |
| Extension | new notes added at the end | the opening |

### Phrases and answers

Many phrases come in pairs. The first phrase (the **question** / call) ends on an unstable point, such as a non-tonic chord tone or a note that wants to resolve. The second (the **answer** / response) ends on a stable point, such as the tonic. This is the melodic counterpart of cadential tension and release.

### Stable and unstable notes

In a major key, scale degrees 1, 3, and 5 are relatively stable and sound at rest over the tonic chord. Degrees 2, 4, 6, and 7 are relatively unstable and tend to move: 7 to 1, 4 to 3, 2 to 1 or 3, 6 to 5. These are tendencies, and they depend on the chord. The note `F` is unstable over `C` but is a chord tone and stable over `F`.

### Space and articulation

Rests are part of the melody. A phrase that never stops gives the listener no place to hear the idea. Articulation on guitar includes:

- **Picked** notes, each with its own attack.
- **Hammer-ons and pull-offs** (legato), where the second note is softer and connected.
- **Slides**, which connect two pitches through the notes between them.
- **Bends**, which raise a pitch continuously (a whole-step bend is two frets of pitch).
- **Vibrato**, a small oscillation on a held note.
- **Muting**, which shortens a note.

## Musical significance

Players are remembered for phrases, not scales. A motif that returns, grows, and resolves gives a solo a story. Melody built from chord tones on strong beats (see improvising through changes) with unstable notes in between creates natural tension and release.

## Guitar application

### A four-bar phrase in `C` major

Chords: `C | G7 | C | C`. Strings are numbered 1 (high `E`) and 2 (`B`).

| Bar | Chord | Notes | String/fret | Rhythm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `C` | E G A G | 1/0, 1/3, 1/5, 1/3 | quarter, eighth, eighth, half |
| 2 | `G7` | D F G F | 2/3, 2/6, 2/8, 2/6 | quarter, eighth, eighth, half |
| 3 | `C` | C E F E | 2/1, 2/5, 2/6, 2/5 | quarter, eighth, eighth, half |
| 4 | `C` | C | 2/1 | whole note, with vibrato |

Notice:

- The motif is `E G A G`: up a third, up a step, back down a step, with a long-short-short-long rhythm.
- Bars 2 and 3 are a **sequence**: the same motif moved down a scale step each time (`E G A G`, `D F G F`, `C E F E`).
- Bar 2's `D F G F` fits `G7`: `D` is the fifth, `F` the seventh, `G` the root.
- Bar 2 ends on `F`, an unstable note (the seventh of `G7`), which sounds like a question. Bar 3 ends on `E` and bar 4 resolves to `C`.
- A **diatonic inversion** of the motif `E G A G` in `C` major is `E C B C` (down a third, down a step, up a step): `1/0, 2/1, 2/0, 2/1`.

### Articulation experiments

Play bar 1 three ways: all picked; `E` to `G` as a hammer-on; and the `A` approached by a slide from fret 3 to fret 5. In bar 2, slide from `F` (fret 6) to `G` (fret 8). Try bending `G` on string 2 fret 8 up a whole step to `A` before releasing it back.

### Space

Insert a rest of half a bar between bars 2 and 3. Notice whether the question and answer sound clearer.

## Exercises

1. Learn the phrase in the table, with a metronome at 70 BPM, counting four beats per bar.
2. Write a new motif of four notes in `A` minor pentatonic (`A C D E G`). Then write its sequence down a scale step and its inversion. Check that the rhythm stays the same in the sequence.
3. Take the motif `E G A G` and change only its rhythm (for example, eighth, eighth, quarter, quarter) while keeping the pitches. Record both and say which sounds more like the same idea.
4. Over `Dm7 | G7 | Cmaj7 | Cmaj7`, play a one-bar motif on chord tones of `Dm7`, then repeat it as a sequence on `G7` and again on `Cmaj7`, adjusting pitches to fit each chord. Mark the unstable note in each bar.
5. Play any two-bar phrase and then answer it. Name which note ends the question and which ends the answer, and say why the answer sounds more final.
6. Play a phrase using only one pitch and vary its rhythm, articulation, and dynamics. Describe what remains of the "melody".

## Mastery criteria

- **Conceptual:** define motif, phrase, development, articulation, and tension and release, and distinguish sequence from inversion.
- **Written:** write a four-note motif with rhythm and develop it by sequence and inversion in one key.
- **Fretboard:** play a motif and its sequence over a chord change, shifting by a scale step.
- **Practical:** play the same line with three different articulations at a steady tempo.
- **Aural:** hear whether a phrase ends in a question or an answer, and identify the unstable final note.
- **Application:** improvise a four-bar phrase over a given progression using one motif and at least one development technique, with a clear rest.
