# Working with the metronome

**Prerequisites:** Pulse, meter, and rhythmic notation; subdivisions, syncopation, and feel  
**Next:** Fretboard memorization (practice), then intervals

## Concept

A **metronome** is an external pulse you practice against. The goal is not to sound mechanical forever — it is to **build an internal clock** so your playing stays steady when the click is gone, when you play with other people, or when the arrangement gets busy.

Treat the click as a partner you lock to, not as a judge. If you rush or drag, that is information: your hands want a different tempo than your ear. Slow down until the hands and the ear agree, then raise the tempo in small steps.

Three ideas drive every exercise below:

1. **The pulse stays constant.** Subdivisions change density; tempo does not.
2. **Lock before you accelerate.** Clean at a slower BPM beats messy at a faster one.
3. **Silence is part of rhythm.** Rests and ghost notes must sit on the grid too.

## Underlying logic

### Where the click sits

| Click setting | What you hear | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quarters (every beat) | `1 2 3 4` | Learning lock-in; checking rush/drag |
| Half notes (beats 1 and 3) | Strong beats only | Feeling the larger pulse |
| Beats 2 and 4 | Backbeat click | Rock/pop groove feel |
| Eighths | Click on every `&` | Tightening syncopation |
| One click per bar (beat 1 only) | Sparse anchor | Testing whether your internal pulse holds |

Start with quarters. Move to 2-and-4, then bar-only, once quarters feel automatic.

### How to know you are locked

You are locked when:

- Your attack lands **with** the click (not ahead or behind) for several bars in a row.
- Changing subdivision (quarters → eighths → sixteenths) does not change the tempo.
- You can stop playing for two bars, keep counting, and re-enter on the correct beat.

If the click seems to “jump,” you drifted. Restart that block at a slower BPM.

### Tempo ladder

Use a simple ladder for any exercise:

1. Find a BPM where you can play **8 bars with zero corrections**.
2. Add **4–8 BPM**.
3. Repeat until the target tempo is clean for 8 bars.
4. Drop **20–30 BPM** below target and play the same material as a cool-down — accuracy over heroics.

Never jump more than about 8 BPM at a time on material that still feels shaky.

## Musical significance

Groove, phrasing, and band playing all depend on a shared pulse. Harmony and fretting skill do not fix timing. A simple riff locked to the click will feel more musical than a complex run that rushes the downbeat.

Rhythm practice belongs beside every later stage: scales, chords, improvisation. Assign a subdivision and a BPM whenever you practice pitch material.

## Guitar application

Mute the strings (palm mute or left-hand damp) so pitch does not distract you. Use one open string or one chord only until the rhythm is honest. Then put the same rhythm on scales, progressions, or licks you already know.

Default setup for this lesson:

- Meter: `4/4`
- Starting tempo: **60–72 BPM**
- Sound: muted strums or single-note pick attacks
- Count aloud for the first week of each new exercise

## Exercises

Do these in order. Mastery for an exercise means **two clean 8-bar takes in a row** at the listed tempo (or your current ladder tempo).

### Block A — Body and pulse (no guitar)

1. **Foot pulse.** Set 60 BPM. Tap your foot on every click for 2 minutes. Do not play. Feel the click in your body first.
2. **Clap quarters.** Clap on every beat for 16 bars. Then clap only on **1 and 3** for 16 bars while the click still marks all four.
3. **Clap backbeat.** Clap on **2 and 4** for 16 bars. Notice how the click on 1 and 3 (or every beat) frames the clap.
4. **Silent bars.** Play/clap 2 bars of quarters, rest 2 bars (count silently), re-enter on beat 1. Repeat for 8 cycles. Missed re-entries restart the cycle.

### Block B — Locking the pick hand

5. **Quarters locked.** Mute-strum quarters at 60 BPM for 8 bars. Record yourself if you can; listen for attacks ahead of the click.
6. **Duration ladder at one BPM.** At 66 BPM, play 4 bars each: whole notes → half notes → quarters → eighths → back to quarters. The pulse must not speed up when the notes get shorter.
7. **Accent map.** Eighths at 66 BPM for 8 bars. Accent only beat **1**, then only **2 and 4**, then only the **`&`s**. Same motion; different weight.
8. **Offbeat only.** At 60 BPM, mute-strum only on the `&` of each beat for 8 bars (`& & & &`). Quarters still click. This trains syncopation against a clear pulse.

### Block C — Subdivisions and feel

9. **Triplet grid.** Quarters click at 56 BPM. Play eighth-note triplets (`1-trip-let`) for 8 bars. Count aloud. Restart if any group bunches.
10. **Sixteenth control.** At 50–56 BPM, play `1 e & a` for 4 bars, then quarters for 4 bars. Sixteenths must leave the quarter pulse unchanged.
11. **Straight ↔ swing.** At 72 BPM, two bars straight eighths, two bars swung eighths, repeat for 16 bars. Harmony can be one chord; feel is the only change.
12. **Anticipations.** Quarters at 70 BPM. Play a short muted hit on the **`&` of 4** leading into beat 1 of the next bar (anticipated downbeat). Do this every bar for 8 bars without rushing the following quarter.

### Block D — Sparse click (internal clock)

13. **Click on 1 and 3 only.** Fill beats 2 and 4 yourself with muted quarters for 16 bars at 72 BPM.
14. **Click on 2 and 4 only.** Play a simple chord on beats 1 and 3; let the metronome own the backbeat for 16 bars.
15. **One click per bar.** Metronome on beat 1 only at 80 BPM. Play continuous eighths for 8 bars. If you drift, you will miss the next bar’s click.
16. **Remove the click.** After a clean take of exercise 15, turn the metronome off and play the same 8 bars. Tap a toe or count; then turn the click back on and check whether you landed near the old tempo.

### Block E — Musical material on the grid

17. **Open-string scale rhythm.** Pick any major scale you know on one string. Play it in eighths at a locked BPM for two octaves up and down. Wrong note = restart the bar, not the tempo.
18. **Chord loop.** Play `| G | C | D | G |` as half notes, then as quarters, then as eighths, all at the same BPM. Function stays; density changes.
19. **Lick in a box.** Take a 1- or 2-bar lick. Loop it for 16 bars at a tempo where every attack is with the click. Raise 4 BPM only after two clean loops.
20. **Call and response.** Two bars of a written rhythm (your choice), two bars improvised rhythm on one pitch, repeat. Both halves must stay on the grid.

## Practice routine

### Daily metronome block (15–20 minutes)

Use this as a warm-up before theory or repertoire practice.

| Minutes | Work | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Foot pulse + clap quarters (Block A) | No guitar |
| 5 | Duration ladder + accent map (ex. 6–7) | Mute strings |
| 5 | One hard item from Block C or D | Stay on today’s ladder BPM |
| 3–5 | Musical material (ex. 17 or 18) | Same BPM as the hard item |
| 1 | Cool-down: same material −20 BPM | End clean |

Rules for the daily block:

- **One BPM focus per day** for the hard item. Write it down.
- If you fail twice in a row, drop 8 BPM. Do not “push through” sloppy takes.
- End on a clean take, even if it is slow.

### Three-week progression

| Week | Focus | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Blocks A–B | Quarters and eighths locked at 60–72 BPM; silent-bar re-entry reliable |
| 2 | Block C + start D | Triplets and sixteenths clean at 52–66 BPM; straight/swing switch without tempo drift |
| 3 | Blocks D–E | Bar-only click for 8 bars; one scale and one chord loop honest at a written BPM |

After week 3, keep the daily 15-minute block forever, but rotate the hard item: syncopation one day, sparse click the next, musical material the next.

### Weekly check (once per week, ~10 minutes)

1. Record 8 bars of eighths at your current “clean” BPM.
2. Record the same 8 bars with click on beat 1 only.
3. Record 8 bars with no click, then turn the click on and see if you are early, late, or close.
4. Note the BPM and the result in one line (example: `80 BPM bar-click — slightly early on bars 5–6`).

### Integrating with the rest of the curriculum

Whenever you practice scales, CAGED shapes, triads, or improvisation:

1. Choose a subdivision (usually eighths).
2. Choose a BPM you can lock.
3. Play the pitch material under that constraint.
4. If fretting errors appear, slow the BPM — do not abandon the click.

## Common mistakes

- **Speeding up to “practice at tempo” before the hands are honest.** Fix: ladder down until 8 bars are clean.
- **Only listening for the notes, not the click.** Fix: mute strings for half of every session.
- **Stopping when you rush instead of finishing the bar and diagnosing.** Fix: finish the phrase, then drop BPM.
- **Click on every sixteenth forever.** Fix: graduate to 2-and-4 and bar-only so the internal pulse does real work.
- **Changing three variables at once** (new lick + new subdivision + higher BPM). Fix: change one variable per take.

## Mastery criteria

- **Conceptual:** Explain why a metronome builds an internal pulse, and what “locked” means (attacks with the click; subdivision changes without tempo change).
- **Practical:** Pass exercises 6, 8, 9, and 15 at a documented BPM with two clean 8-bar takes each.
- **Practical:** Switch straight and swung eighths for 16 bars without drifting (exercise 11).
- **Aural:** Hear whether you are ahead of or behind the click when you listen back to a recording.
- **Application:** Practice any scale or chord loop for 8 bars under a chosen subdivision and BPM, then remove the click for 8 bars and stay near that tempo.
- **Habit:** Complete the 15-minute daily metronome block at least five days in a week for three weeks.
