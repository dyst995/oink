# Seventh chords

**Prerequisites:** Triads  
**Next:** Diatonic harmony

## Concept

A **seventh chord** adds one more third above a triad: root, third, fifth, seventh. Four pitch classes. The seventh’s quality (relative to the root’s major scale) creates the main types you need on guitar:

| Name | Formula | Think of it as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Major 7 | `1 3 5 7` | Major triad + major 7th |
| Dominant 7 | `1 3 5 ♭7` | Major triad + minor 7th |
| Minor 7 | `1 ♭3 5 ♭7` | Minor triad + minor 7th |
| Half-diminished (m7♭5) | `1 ♭3 ♭5 ♭7` | Diminished triad + minor 7th |
| Diminished 7 | `1 ♭3 ♭5 ♭♭7` | Diminished triad + diminished 7th |

Symbols you will see: `Cmaj7` / `CΔ7`, `C7` (dominant), `Cm7`, `Cm7♭5` / `Cø`, `Cdim7` / `C°7`.

**Dominant 7** is the tension chord of tonal music. The major third and minor seventh form a tritone that wants to resolve (toward the tonic chord of a key a fifth below — covered next with V→I). Other sevenths color a harmony; dominant 7 actively pulls.

Same spelling rule as triads: stack letters in thirds, then apply accidentals for the formula.

## Examples

- `Gmaj7`: `G B D F♯`.
- `G7` (dominant): `G B D F`.
- `Gm7`: `G B♭ D F`.
- `Bm7♭5`: `B D F A` — the viiø7 of C major (preview).
- `Bdim7`: `B D F A♭`.

## Exercises

1. Spell `maj7`, `7`, and `m7` on `A` and on `E♭`.
2. From `C7` (`C E G B♭`), name the tritone interval between the 3rd and ♭7th.
3. Play a G major triad, then add `F♯` (maj7) and separately `F` (dominant 7). Hear which version wants to move more.

**Done when:** you can build maj7, dominant 7, m7, and m7♭5 from formulas, and you can explain dominant 7 as major triad + ♭7 with forward pull.
