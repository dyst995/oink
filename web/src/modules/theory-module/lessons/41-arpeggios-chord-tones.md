# Arpeggios and chord tones

**Prerequisites:** Triad arpeggios; sevenths; functional progressions  
**Next:** Improvising through chord changes

## Concept

An **arpeggio** is a chord played one note at a time. In lead playing, arpeggios are a way to **outline the changes**: when you play the chord tones of each chord as the harmony passes, the listener hears the chords inside your melody, even without a rhythm guitar.

**Targeting** is the skill of arriving on a chosen chord tone at a chord change, often the third or seventh, so the line points clearly at the harmony.

## Underlying logic

### Chord tones carry the harmony

Chord tones are the notes of the chord. The **third** and **seventh** decide the quality (lessons 25 and 32), so landing on them is the clearest way to state a chord. The root and fifth are stable and supporting: the root restates the center, and the fifth adds stability.

A non-chord note on a strong beat creates tension against the chord. That is not wrong; it is a different effect. For clear outlining, put chord tones on strong beats and let other notes lead into them.

### Chord tones for common chords

| Chord | Tones |
| --- | --- |
| `Dm7` | D F A C |
| `G7` | G B D F |
| `Cmaj7` | C E G B |
| `A7` | A C♯ E G |
| `D7` | D F♯ A C |
| `E7` | E G♯ B D |

### Connecting arpeggios

At a chord change, find the closest chord tone of the **new** chord to where your line currently ends. The best connection is usually one fret or one scale step. In a `ii V I`, the seventh of `Dm7` (`C`) is a half step above `B`, the third of `G7`, and the third of `Dm7` (`F`) is the seventh of `G7`.

Arpeggio shapes are small and portable. They are not separate "licks": each is a subset of the scale pattern you are already in, since the tones of a seventh chord are every other note of its scale.

## Musical significance

Arpeggios give melody a clear harmonic backbone. Many strong melodic lines are mostly chord tones, with scale notes as passing notes between them. Outlining the changes is also the basis of walking basslines and many solos.

## Guitar application

### A line on strings 2 and 1 (`ii V I` in `C`)

Using only the top two strings, here is a one-bar-per-chord line. Notes are given as string/fret.

| Chord | Notes in order | String/fret | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Dm7` | D F A C | 2/3, 2/6, 1/5, 1/8 | root, 3rd, 5th, 7th |
| `G7` | B G F D | 1/7, 1/3, 2/6, 2/3 | 3rd, root, 7th, 5th |
| `Cmaj7` | E | 2/5 | 3rd |

Listen for the targeting: the last `Dm7` note (`C`, string 1 fret 8) steps down a half step to `B` (string 1 fret 7), the third of `G7`. The line descends through `G7` and ends on `D` (string 2 fret 3), which steps up a whole step to `E` (string 2 fret 5), the third of `Cmaj7`.

### A blues line in `A`

Over `A7` to `D7`, end the `A7` idea on `G` (the seventh) and step to `F♯` (the third of `D7`). On string 1: `G` is fret 3 and `F♯` is fret 2.

From `D7` to `E7`, end the `D7` idea on `A` (its fifth, string 1 fret 5) and step down a half step to `G♯` (the third of `E7`, string 1 fret 4).

Both are the same move: one fret toward the new chord's third.

### Practice order

1. One chord: ascending and descending arpeggio in one position.
2. Two chords: end one arpeggio on a chord tone, begin the next on the closest chord tone of the next chord.
3. A whole progression, one chord per bar, with a steady pulse.
4. Rest between arpeggios and play each as a phrase (see melody, motif, and phrasing).

## Exercises

1. Play `Dm7`, `G7`, and `Cmaj7` arpeggios ascending in one region of the neck. Name each note.
2. Play the line in the table above at a slow tempo. Name the target at each change (`B`, then `E`).
3. Over `A7 D7 E7` (bars 1, 5, and 9 of a blues), play one arpeggio per chord in any position and mark the third on each change.
4. For `Am7 D7 Gmaj7`, write each chord's tones and find a one-fret or one-step connection between the end of each arpeggio and the start of the next. Answer: `Am7` ends on `G` (seventh), and `F♯`, the third of `D7`, is a half step below it. `D7`'s seventh `C` then steps down to `B`, the third of `Gmaj7`.
5. Record a short loop of `ii V I` in `C` and play only the thirds, one per chord. Name them: `F`, `B`, `E`.

## Mastery criteria

- **Conceptual:** explain how chord tones outline a chord and why the third and seventh carry the quality.
- **Written:** write the tones of any seventh chord in a progression.
- **Fretboard:** play an arpeggio of a given seventh chord in two different positions.
- **Fretboard:** connect two arpeggios by a half step or whole step.
- **Aural:** hear the third of the new chord arrive on the change.
- **Application:** play an outline of a short progression so the changes are audible without any accompaniment.
