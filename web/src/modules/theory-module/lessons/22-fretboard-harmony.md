# Fretboard harmony and voice leading

**Prerequisites:** Functional progressions; chord shapes; arpeggios  
**Next:** Pulse, subdivision, and feel

## Concept

**Voice leading** connects chords by moving each tone as little as possible. On guitar that means preferring small fret/string shifts over jumping to a distant grip of the next chord.

**Guide tones** for seventh chords are mainly the 3rd and 7th — they define quality and dominate the V→I resolution (3 of V leads to 1 of I; ♭7 of V leads to 3 of I).

**Shell voicings** keep essential tones (often root + 3 + 7, sometimes omitting the fifth) so changes stay clear and movable.

Think in voices: where does each finger’s pitch go next? Parallel jumps of the whole shape are fine for rhythm guitar; connected leading is what makes progressions sing.

## Examples

- `G7` → `Cmaj7`: move `B`→`C`, `F`→`E`, keep or shift `G` gently.
- ii–V–I shells on the D/G/B strings: keep middle voices stepwise where possible.
- Two grips of the same chord that share two pitch classes — switch using the shared tones as pivots.

## Exercises

1. Voice-lead `| Dm7 | G7 | Cmaj7 |` in one neck area. Write or say each chord’s 3 and 7.
2. Play only guide tones (3 and 7) through that progression. Then add roots.
3. Take I–IV–V–I in G. Connect the chords with the smallest hand motion you can; avoid open-position jumps unless they truly are nearest.

**Done when:** you can move through a short progression on the neck with mostly stepwise or common-tone connections and point to the guide-tone resolution on V→I.
