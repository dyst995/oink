# Diatonic triads

**Prerequisites:** Triads on string sets; keys and scale degrees  
**Next:** Triad voice leading

## Concept

A **diatonic triad** is a triad built on a scale degree using only notes of the key. To **harmonize** a scale, stack thirds on each degree, skipping every other scale note: degree, degree plus two, degree plus four.

In a major key the seven triads have this pattern:

| Degree | Roman numeral | Quality | Triad in `C` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `I` | Major | `C E G` |
| 2 | `ii` | Minor | `D F A` |
| 3 | `iii` | Minor | `E G B` |
| 4 | `IV` | Major | `F A C` |
| 5 | `V` | Major | `G B D` |
| 6 | `vi` | Minor | `A C E` |
| 7 | `vii°` | Diminished | `B D F` |

**Roman numerals** name chords by the scale degree of their root. Uppercase means major, lowercase means minor, and `°` means diminished. They stay the same in every key: `I – IV – V` is `C F G` in `C`, `G C D` in `G`.

## Underlying logic

The qualities are not memorized; they follow from the scale. The major scale's whole and half steps make thirds of different sizes on different degrees:

| Degree | Third above the root | Fifth above the third | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | M3 (`C E`) | m3 (`E G`) | Major |
| 2 | m3 (`D F`) | M3 (`F A`) | Minor |
| 3 | m3 (`E G`) | M3 (`G B`) | Minor |
| 4 | M3 (`F A`) | m3 (`A C`) | Major |
| 5 | M3 (`G B`) | m3 (`B D`) | Major |
| 6 | m3 (`A C`) | M3 (`C E`) | Minor |
| 7 | m3 (`B D`) | m3 (`D F`) | Diminished |

The two half steps of the scale (`E–F` and `B–C` in `C` major) shrink the thirds that span them. Thirds containing a half step are minor: `D–F`, `E–G`, `A–C`, `B–D`. The rest are major: `C–E`, `F–A`, `G–B`. Two minor thirds in a row (`B–D`, `D–F`) give the diminished triad. Any major key has three major triads (`I IV V`), three minor (`ii iii vi`), and one diminished (`vii°`).

**Spell each chord from the key signature, not from a fixed chord.** In `G` major (one sharp, `F♯`), `vii°` is `F♯ A C`, because the chord uses `F♯`. Do not write `F A C` just because `F A C` is common in `C` major.

| Key | `I` | `ii` | `iii` | `IV` | `V` | `vi` | `vii°` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `C` | `C` | `Dm` | `Em` | `F` | `G` | `Am` | `Bdim` |
| `G` | `G` | `Am` | `Bm` | `C` | `D` | `Em` | `F♯dim` |
| `D` | `D` | `Em` | `F♯m` | `G` | `A` | `Bm` | `C♯dim` |
| `A` | `A` | `Bm` | `C♯m` | `D` | `E` | `F♯m` | `G♯dim` |
| `F` | `F` | `Gm` | `Am` | `B♭` | `C` | `Dm` | `Edim` |
| `B♭` | `B♭` | `Cm` | `Dm` | `E♭` | `F` | `Gm` | `Adim` |

**Natural minor preview.** Harmonize `A` natural minor (`A B C D E F G`):

| Degree | Roman numeral | Triad in `A` minor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `i` | `Am` (`A C E`) |
| 2 | `ii°` | `Bdim` (`B D F`) |
| 3 | `III` | `C` (`C E G`) |
| 4 | `iv` | `Dm` (`D F A`) |
| 5 | `v` | `Em` (`E G B`) |
| 6 | `VI` | `F` (`F A C`) |
| 7 | `VII` | `G` (`G B D`) |

It is the same seven chords as `C` major with the numbering rotated to start on `A`. The chord on degree 5 is minor; with the raised 7 (harmonic minor) it becomes major `E`, written `V`.

## Musical significance

The chords group by function:

- **Tonic** (rest): `I`, with `vi` (and sometimes `iii`) as substitutes sharing two notes with `I`.
- **Predominant** (departure): `IV` and `ii`, which lead toward V.
- **Dominant** (tension): `V`, and `vii°`, which contains the leading tone and the `4`.

`I`, `IV`, and `V` are the **primary triads**. Together they contain every note of the major scale: in `C`, `C E G`, `F A C`, `G B D` give `C D E F G A B`. Many songs use only these three plus `vi`.

The diminished `vii°` (`B D F`) contains `B`, the leading tone, and `F`, the `4`, which together form a tritone that pulls toward `C`: `B` to `C`, `F` to `E`. It behaves like a `V7` without a root.

## Guitar application

Place all seven `C` major triads in root position on strings 4–3–2 (`D G B`). The shapes come from lesson 21's patterns. Ascending in pitch from `F`:

| Chord | Numeral | String 4 | String 3 | String 2 | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `F` | `IV` | 3 | 2 | 1 | major |
| `G` | `V` | 5 | 4 | 3 | major |
| `Am` | `vi` | 7 | 5 | 5 | minor |
| `Bdim` | `vii°` | 9 | 7 | 6 | diminished |
| `C` | `I` | 10 | 9 | 8 | major |
| `Dm` | `ii` | 12 | 10 | 10 | minor |
| `Em` | `iii` | 14 | 12 | 12 | minor |

Observe the pattern: reading from the bass string, the frets are `r, r−1, r−2` for major, `r, r−2, r−2` for minor, `r, r−2, r−3` for diminished. A single string (string 4) climbs through the scale (`F G A B C D E`), and the quality shows in how the other two frets sit relative to it. Play this ladder up and down and say the numeral aloud for each.

The same method works in any key: find the seven root notes of the scale on string 4, then apply the pattern for that numeral's quality. For `G` major, string 4 roots `G A B C D E F♯` at frets `5 7 9 10 12 14 16`, with major shapes on `G C D`, minor on `Am Bm Em`, and diminished on `F♯`.

Hearing the qualities: play `C – Am – F – G`. Name each numeral (`I vi IV V`) and say whether it is major or minor.

## Exercises

1. Harmonize `D` major and write the Roman numerals. Expected: `D Em F♯m G A Bm C♯dim` as `I ii iii IV V vi vii°`.
2. Harmonize `E` major. Expected: `E F♯m G♯m A B C♯m D♯dim`.
3. Harmonize `B♭` major and `F` major. Expected: `B♭ Cm Dm E♭ F Gm Adim` and `F Gm Am B♭ C Dm Edim`.
4. Harmonize `D` natural minor. Expected: `Dm Edim F Gm Am B♭ C` as `i ii° III iv v VI VII`.
5. Which chord is `iii` in `A` major? `V` in `E` major? `IV` in `B♭` major? `vi` in `G` major? Expected: `C♯m`, `B`, `E♭`, `Em`.
6. Convert `C – Am – F – G` to Roman numerals, then play it in `G` and in `D`. Expected: `I vi IV V`; in `G`: `G Em C D`; in `D`: `D Bm G A`.
7. On strings 4–3–2, play the `C` major ladder from the table, `F G Am Bdim C Dm Em`, naming root and quality of each.
8. Play `C – G – Am – F`, then `Am – F – C – G`. Describe where each progression feels settled. Both use the same four chords.
9. Build the `G` major ladder on strings 4–3–2 from root frets `5 7 9 10 12 14 16`. Expected shapes: `G 5 4 3`, `Am 7 5 5`, `Bm 9 7 7`, `C 10 9 8`, `D 12 11 10`, `Em 14 12 12`, `F♯dim 16 14 13`.

## Mastery criteria

- **Conceptual:** Explain why the quality pattern `M m m M M m dim` appears in every major key, using the sizes of the stacked thirds.
- **Written:** Harmonize any major key up to four accidentals with correct spelling and numerals, and the natural minor of those keys.
- **Fretboard:** Play the seven diatonic triads of a major key in root position on strings 4–3–2 as a ladder, naming numeral and quality of each shape.
- **Aural:** Distinguish `I`, `IV`, and `V` from `ii`, `iii`, and `vi` by quality, and hear that `vii°` is unstable.
- **Application:** Transpose a progression from Roman numerals to any key.
