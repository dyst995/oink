# Diatonic harmony

**Prerequisites:** Scale degrees and formulas; seventh chords  
**Next:** Functional progressions

## Concept

**Diatonic** chords are built only from the pitch classes of one key. Harmonize a major scale by stacking thirds on each degree, staying inside that scale.

Using seventh chords (four notes), the qualities in every major key are fixed by degree:

| Degree | Roman numeral | Quality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Imaj7 | Major 7 |
| 2 | iim7 | Minor 7 |
| 3 | iiim7 | Minor 7 |
| 4 | IVmaj7 | Major 7 |
| 5 | V7 | Dominant 7 |
| 6 | vim7 | Minor 7 |
| 7 | viiø7 | Half-diminished (m7♭5) |

**Roman numerals** label the degree of the chord’s root in the key. Uppercase often marks major/dominant quality; lowercase marks minor/diminished. The pattern above is the same in every major key — only the pitch classes change with the tonic.

Triads alone (no sevenths) follow the same degrees with qualities: I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii°. Sevenths are the fuller working set for electric guitar and most popular harmony.

Why derive it instead of memorizing a chart: if you can build the major scale and stack thirds, you can rebuild the chord set in any key on demand.

## Examples

- In `C` major: `Cmaj7`, `Dm7`, `Em7`, `Fmaj7`, `G7`, `Am7`, `Bm7♭5`.
- In `G` major: `Gmaj7`, `Am7`, `Bm7`, `Cmaj7`, `D7`, `Em7`, `F♯m7♭5`.
- Same progression `IVmaj7 – V7 – Imaj7` in C is `Fmaj7 – G7 – Cmaj7`; in A it is `Dmaj7 – E7 – Amaj7`.

## Exercises

1. Write all seven diatonic seventh chords for `D` major with chord symbols and Roman numerals.
2. In `F` major, which chord is V7? Spell it. Which is iim7? Spell it.
3. Take `Am7 – D7 – Gmaj7`. Name a major key where this is ii–V–I (by quality and root movement).

**Done when:** you can derive I–viiø7 for any major key from the scale, and you can label a diatonic chord with a Roman numeral without looking at a poster.
