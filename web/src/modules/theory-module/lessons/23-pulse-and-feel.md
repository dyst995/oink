# Pulse, subdivision, and feel

**Prerequisites:** Basic counting  
**Next:** Keep applying this against Unit D–E material (same chords, clearer time)

## Concept

**Pulse** is the steady beat you can tap — the clock under the music. A **bar** (measure) groups beats under a meter; in common time (`4/4`) you feel four pulses per bar, often counted `1 2 3 4`.

**Subdivision** splits each beat into equal parts without changing the pulse:

| Against one beat | Count (one beat) | Feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quarter (the beat itself) | `1` | One hit per pulse |
| Eighths | `1 &` | Two equal parts |
| Triplets | `1-trip-let` | Three equal parts |
| Sixteenths | `1 e & a` | Four equal parts |

The pulse stays the same size; only how many equal slices fit inside it changes. Mixing subdivisions (e.g. eighths then sixteenths) is still locked to that pulse.

**Straight** eighths divide the beat evenly. **Swing** (common in blues/jazz feels) keeps the same written eighths but stretches the first of each pair and shortens the second — closer to a long–short triplet feel (`1` long, `trip` silent, `let` short) without rewriting the harmony. Feel is how those slices are weighted, not which chords you chose.

On guitar: mute-strum, single-note picking, or a one-chord vamp all work as vehicles. Time is independent of pitch choices — the same `I–IV–V` can sit in straight eighths or a swung groove.

## Examples

- Metronome at 80 BPM = pulse. Count `1 2 3 4` with it; each click is a quarter.
- Same tempo: pick eighths on an open string — two notes per click, saying `1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &`.
- Same tempo: sixteenths — four notes per click, `1 e & a 2 e & a …`.
- Same tempo: triplets — three notes per click.
- Straight vs swing: play a muted `A7` vamp in eighths even, then lean the `&` later so the pair feels long–short while the click stays on `1 2 3 4`.

## Exercises

1. With a metronome, clap or mute-strum quarters for eight bars of `4/4`, then eighths for eight, then sixteenths for four — without drifting off the click. Restart if you rush or drag.
2. At a moderate tempo, play any single pitch class you know on the neck: one bar quarters, one bar eighths, one bar triplets, one bar sixteenths. Count out loud.
3. Hold one chord (or muted strings). Play eight bars straight eighths, then eight bars swung eighths at the same BPM. The harmony must stay identical; only the feel changes.

**Done when:** you can lock quarters, eighths, triplets, and sixteenths to a steady pulse and switch straight vs swing without changing what chord or note you’re playing.
