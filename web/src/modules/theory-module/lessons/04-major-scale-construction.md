# Constructing the major scale

**Prerequisites:** Half steps and whole steps  
**Next:** Keys and tonality

## Concept

A **major scale** is not a memorized list of notes. It is a **recipe of steps** from a starting pitch class (the root):

**W – W – H – W – W – W – H**

W = whole step, H = half step. Apply that pattern from any root and you get that root’s major scale.

Why this pattern: it picks seven of the twelve classes so that (when spelled correctly) each scale degree gets its **own letter**. From `C` you get `C D E F G A B` — seven letters, no accidentals. From other roots you will need sharps or flats, but still one letter per step of the alphabet.

Spelling rule while you build: after the root, use the **next letter** for each new degree, then add `♯` or `♭` so the distance matches the recipe. Do not jump letters to avoid accidentals. That keeps degrees honest for keys and chords later.

You do not need the full fretboard map to build a scale on paper. One string on the guitar is enough to hear the recipe: W = two frets, H = one fret.

## Examples

- From `C`: W→`D`, W→`E`, H→`F`, W→`G`, W→`A`, W→`B`, H→`C`.  
  Result: `C D E F G A B (C)`.
- From `G`: W→`A`, W→`B`, H→`C`, W→`D`, W→`E`, W→`F♯` (not `G♭`), H→`G`.  
  Result: `G A B C D E F♯ (G)`.
- From `F`: W→`G`, W→`A`, H→`B♭` (not `A♯`), W→`C`, W→`D`, W→`E`, H→`F`.  
  Result: `F G A B♭ C D E (F)`.

## Exercises

1. Build the major scale from `D` using only W/H counts. Spell each degree with the next letter. Check: you should need `F♯` and `C♯`.
2. Build from `B♭`. Check: the scale should use flats, not a mix that duplicates letters.
3. On any open string, play W–W–H–W–W–W–H from the open pitch. Say “whole” or “half” as you move. Then name the pitch classes if you can; if not, the step feel still counts.

**Done when:** given any root, you can produce its major scale from the WWHWWWH recipe and justify each accidental by letter-plus-distance, not by guesswork.
