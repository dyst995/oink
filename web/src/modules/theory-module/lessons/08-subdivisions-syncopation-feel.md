# Subdivisions, syncopation, and feel

**Prerequisites:** Pulse, meter, and rhythmic notation  
**Next:** Fretboard memorization (practice), then intervals

## Concept

**Subdivision** splits each beat into equal parts without changing the pulse:

| Against one beat | Count | Parts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quarter (the beat) | `1` | 1 |
| Eighths | `1 &` | 2 |
| Triplets | `1-trip-let` | 3 |
| Sixteenths | `1 e & a` | 4 |

**Syncopation** accents weak parts of the beat or bar (the `&` of 2, beat 4 into 1, etc.) so the rhythm pushes against the expected strong beats.

**Straight** eighths are even. **Swing** lengthens the first of an eighth pair and shortens the second (often near a long–short triplet feel). Feel is weighting of the grid, not a change of chords.

## Underlying logic

The pulse stays constant; only the grid density and accent pattern change. Syncopation needs a clear pulse underneath — without it, “offbeat” has nothing to be off.

## Musical significance

Riffs, funk strums, and lead phrasing live in subdivision and syncopation. Straight vs swing changes style identity instantly on the same pitches.

## Guitar application

Mute-strum or pick one chord:

1. Eighths straight for 4 bars, then sixteenths for 2 bars, at one BPM.
2. Accent only the `&`s for 4 bars (syncopation).
3. Same eighth notes swung for 4 bars — harmony unchanged.

Reinforce later: when you practice scales or triads, assign a subdivision (e.g. eighths) and keep it honest to the click.

## Exercises

1. Eight bars quarters → eight eighths → four sixteenths at one tempo; restart if you rush.
2. Play a I chord vamp: two bars straight eighths, two bars swung, same BPM.
3. Write a 1-bar syncopated pattern in `4/4` using eighths that avoids attacking on beat 1; play it.
4. Over any open-string drone, improvise rhythms only (one pitch) for 8 bars using at least two subdivisions.

## Mastery criteria

- **Practical:** Lock eighths, triplets, and sixteenths to a pulse.
- **Application:** Switch straight vs swing without changing harmony.
- **Conceptual:** Define syncopation as accent against expected strong beats.
