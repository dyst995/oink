## Concept

A **cadence** is a harmonic (and usually melodic) arrival that creates a sense of pause or close — like punctuation.

Common tonal cadence types (major-key defaults):

| Cadence | Typical motion | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authentic (perfect/imperfect) | `V → I` (often `V7 → I`) | Strong arrival at tonic |
| Half | arrives on `V` | Open; expects continuation |
| Plagal | `IV → I` | Amen-like close; softer than V–I |
| Deceptive | `V → vi` (not I) | Surprise; postpones tonic |

**Harmonic function** groups chords by job: tonic (stability), predominant (prepares dominant), dominant (tension toward tonic). Cadences are where those jobs land.

Function is a powerful model for tonal music — not a law that every genre obeys equally.

## Underlying logic

`V` (and especially `V7`) contains the leading tone and often the tritone that resolves into tonic chord tones. That is why authentic cadences feel conclusive. A half cadence stops on that tension. Deceptive motion keeps the dominant’s energy but lands on `vi` instead of `I`.

Voice leading clarifies cadences: leading tone up to tonic, tendency tones resolving, bass motion by fifth for authentic close.

## Musical significance

Recognizing cadences lets you hear form (phrase endings), predict resolutions, and write progressions that breathe. Improvisers treat cadential bars as places to resolve or deliberately delay resolution.

## Guitar application

In `G` major, play with voice-led triads on one string set:

- Authentic: `| D | G |` (V–I)
- Half: `| G | C | D |` ending on `D`
- Plagal: `| C | G |`
- Deceptive: `| D | Em |`

Sing the soprano (top string) through each and notice which feels closed.

Ear (with guitar as reference, not a blind test): play V–I vs V–vi and describe the difference.

## Exercises

1. In `C` major, write one example progression (RN + chord symbols) for each cadence type above.
2. On strings 4–3–2, voice-lead `G7` shell or `G` triad into `C` and into `Am`; name the cadence types.
3. Find a song phrase ending; label it authentic, half, plagal, or deceptive (best guess + justification).
4. Compose 4 bars that end in a half cadence, then 4 bars that answer with an authentic cadence in the same key.

## Mastery criteria

- **Conceptual:** Define the four cadence types and relate them to function without claiming universality across all styles.
- **Written:** Build examples of each cadence in any major key.
- **Practical/Aural:** Play and recognize V–I vs V–vi vs arrival on V on guitar.
