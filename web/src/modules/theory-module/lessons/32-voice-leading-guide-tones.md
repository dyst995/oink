# Voice leading and guide tones

**Prerequisites:** Sevenths on guitar; functional progressions; chord substitutions  
**Next:** CAGED navigation

## Concept

**Voice leading** is how the individual notes of one chord move into the next. Good voice leading keeps most notes common or close, so the progression sounds smooth even when the chord roots jump.

**Guide tones** are the third and seventh of each chord. They carry quality and function, so tracing them across a progression shows the harmony more clearly than tracing roots. On guitar, minimal motion means finding voicings in which the notes move by one or two frets, or stay put, as the chords change.

## Underlying logic

### Common tones and smallest steps

Between two chords, apply these steps in order:

1. **Hold common tones.** If a pitch class belongs to both chords, keep it on the same string and fret when you can.
2. **Move the remaining voices by the smallest step.** A half step or whole step is best.
3. **Resolve the tensions.** Leading tones usually rise, and sevenths usually fall. These are tendencies, not absolutes.

### Guide tones across `ii V I`

In `C` major:

| Chord | Third | Seventh |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Dm7` | `F` | `C` |
| `G7` | `B` | `F` |
| `Cmaj7` | `E` | `B` |

Follow each line:

- Seventh of `Dm7` (`C`) to third of `G7` (`B`): down a half step.
- Third of `G7` (`B`) to seventh of `Cmaj7` (`B`): common tone.
- Third of `Dm7` (`F`) to seventh of `G7` (`F`): common tone.
- Seventh of `G7` (`F`) to third of `Cmaj7` (`E`): down a half step.

Two lines (`C B B` and `F F E`) carry the whole chord sequence. The same pattern holds in any key for a major `ii V I` with seventh chords.

### In a minor ii V i

In `C` minor: `Dm7♭5` (third `F`, seventh `C`), `G7` (`B`, `F`), `Cm7` (`E♭`, `B♭`). The lines are `C B B♭` and `F F E♭`. The first line moves by half step twice. The second holds `F`, then falls a whole step to `E♭`.

### In a blues change

From `A7` (third `C♯`, seventh `G`) to `D7` (third `F♯`, seventh `C`), both guide tones fall by half step: the seventh of `A7` (`G`) falls to the third of `D7` (`F♯`), and the third of `A7` (`C♯`) falls to the seventh of `D7` (`C` natural). Two half-step slides turn one dominant 7 into the next.

### Why bass lines matter

Minimal motion applies to the upper voices; the bass usually carries the roots and may jump. The bass note can also change how a chord is heard (lesson 26), so voice leading and bass note are separate choices.

## Musical significance

Smooth voice leading makes guitar comping sound deliberate and connected, because the ear follows notes more readily than chord names. It is also the key to **playing through changes** in lead playing: if you know where the third and seventh are, you can outline the changes by landing on those tones (lessons 35 and 36).

## Guitar application

### Shells for `ii V I` in `C`

| Chord | Frets (6 to 1) | Notes (low to high) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Dm7` | `x5x56x` | D, C, F |
| `G7` | `3xx46x` | G, B, F |
| `Cmaj7` | `x3x45x` | C, B, E |

The top two notes (strings 3 and 2) follow the guide tones: `C F` to `B F` to `B E`, which is frets `5/6`, `4/6`, `4/5`.

### Minimal-motion exercise

Play the three shells in order and count how far the upper two fingers move. Across the whole `ii V I` the only changes on strings 3 and 2 are `C` to `B` (one fret) and `F` to `E` (one fret). The bass is free to jump because the roots move in fifths.

### Blues in `A` with guide tones

Shells with the root on string 6 and string 5:

- `A7`: `5x56xx` (`A G C♯`; string 6 fret 5, string 4 fret 5, string 3 fret 6).
- `D7`: `x5x57x` (`D C F♯`; string 5 fret 5, string 3 fret 5, string 2 fret 7).

The guide tones sit on different strings in the two shells, so track pitch classes rather than frets: `G` falls to `F♯`, and `C♯` falls to `C`.

## Exercises

1. Trace the guide tones of `Em7 A7 Dmaj7` (`ii V I` in `D`). Answer: thirds `G C♯ F♯`, sevenths `D G C♯`. Lines: `D C♯ C♯` and `G G F♯`.
2. Trace the guide tones of `Am7 D7 Gmaj7` (in `G`). Answer: thirds `C F♯ B`, sevenths `G C F♯`. Lines: `G F♯ F♯` and `C C B`.
3. Play the `ii V I` shells above in `C`, then move each shell up two frets for `D` major. Check against: `Em7 x7x78x`, `A7 5xx68x`, `Dmaj7 x5x67x`.
4. Play `A7` shell (`5x56xx`) to `D7` shell (`x5x57x`) and name each guide-tone move (`G` to `F♯`, `C♯` to `C`).
5. For `Cmaj7 Am7 Dm7 G7` (`I vi ii V`), list the notes shared by each neighboring pair. Answer: `Cmaj7` to `Am7` share `C E G`; `Am7` to `Dm7` share `A C`; `Dm7` to `G7` share `F`, and `C` falls to `B`.

## Mastery criteria

- **Conceptual:** define voice leading and guide tones, and explain why guide tones are more informative than roots.
- **Written:** write the guide-tone lines for a major `ii V I` and a minor `ii V i` in any key.
- **Fretboard:** play shells for `ii V I` in at least two keys with minimal finger motion.
- **Fretboard:** find the third and seventh of a dominant 7 chord on strings 6 or 5 without a chart.
- **Aural:** hear a half-step guide-tone resolution when the chords change.
- **Application:** play a `ii V I` or a blues in which the guide-tone line is audible as a melody.
