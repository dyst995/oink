# Pulse and feel

**Prerequisites:** Basic counting; a metronome or backing loop  
**Next:** Melody, motif, and phrasing

## Concept

**Pulse** is the steady beat you can tap. A **bar** (measure) groups a fixed number of beats, set by the meter. In `4/4` you count four beats per bar: `1 2 3 4`. **Tempo** is the speed of the pulse, measured in beats per minute (BPM).

**Subdivision** splits each beat into equal parts. **Feel** is how those parts are weighted and placed: evenly (**straight**) or with a long-short lilt (**swing**). Feel changes the sound of the music without changing the chords or the notes.

## Underlying logic

### Counting subdivisions of one beat

| Subdivision | Count | Parts per beat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quarter notes | `1` | 1 |
| Eighth notes | `1 &` | 2 |
| Eighth-note triplets | `1 trip let` | 3 |
| Sixteenth notes | `1 e & a` | 4 |

The pulse does not change when you change subdivision. You fit more or fewer equal notes inside the same beat.

### Straight and swung eighths

**Straight eighths** divide the beat into two equal halves. **Swung eighths** place the second eighth later in the beat, so the pair sounds long-short. A common approximation is the first and third parts of an eighth-note triplet (a 2:1 ratio). In practice the ratio varies with tempo and style: at faster tempos swing often moves closer to even, and at slower tempos it can be more lopsided. Treat 2:1 as a starting point, not a measurement.

A **shuffle**, as in many blues grooves, is a swung-eighth feel played consistently on every beat.

### Backbeat and strong beats

In `4/4`, beats 1 and 3 usually feel strong. In much blues, rock, and funk, beats 2 and 4 are the **backbeat**. Feeling 2 and 4 is as important as feeling 1.

### Other meters

- `3/4`: three beats per bar (`1 2 3`), as in waltzes.
- `6/8`: two beats per bar, each divided into three (`1 2 3 4 5 6`, with stress on `1` and `4`). A bar of `3/4` also holds six eighth notes, but they group as 2+2+2 (three beats each split in two), so it feels different.
- `12/8`: four beats each divided into three, a common feel for slow blues.

## Musical significance

Pulse is what makes harmony and melody feel organized. The same chord progression played in straight eighths and in a shuffle can sound like two different styles. Good time is often more audible than good note choice: a simple line played with a steady pulse and consistent feel sounds more convincing than a complex line that drifts.

## Guitar application

### Right-hand skills

- **Muted strumming:** lay the fretting hand across the strings and strum, so only rhythm is audible.
- **Alternate picking:** down on the beat, up on the `&` (the off-beat).
- **Dead notes and rests:** silence is part of the rhythm. Stop the strings clearly.

### One pattern in two feels

On a muted `A7` shape (`x02020`), strum quarter notes for a bar, then straight eighths (`1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &`), then swung eighths (long-short on each pair), with the click on `1 2 3 4` the whole time.

A common strum pattern in `4/4` keeps the strumming hand moving down on every beat and up on every `&`, but only touches the strings on some of them. Counting `1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &`, strum on `1`, `2`, `2&`, `3&`, `4`, `4&` (down, down, up, up, down, up) and miss the strings on `1&` and `3`. The hand keeps moving through the misses, so the pulse stays even.

### Feel with a chord progression

Play the same progression, `Dm7 G7 Cmaj7 Cmaj7`, in three ways: straight eighths, swung eighths, and a slow `12/8`-style triplet feel. Do not change the chords.

## Exercises

1. With a metronome at 70 BPM, play eight bars of quarter notes on a muted string, then eight bars of eighths, then four bars of triplets, then four bars of sixteenths. Stay on the click.
2. At 80 BPM, set the click on beats 2 and 4 only (or use a drum loop). Play steady quarters and then eighths while feeling the backbeat.
3. Play a one-chord vamp (for example `A7`) for eight bars straight and eight bars swung at the same BPM. Record both and compare.
4. Play a one-octave `C` major scale (string 5 fret 3 up to string 3 fret 5) in quarters, then straight eighths, then swung eighths, all at the same BPM.
5. Count `1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &` out loud while playing a one-bar rhythm with rests on beats 2 and 4. Check that the rests are silent and the pulse does not stop.

## Mastery criteria

- **Conceptual:** define pulse, bar, subdivision, straight, and swing, and say what changes between straight and swung eighths (and what does not).
- **Conceptual:** explain why 2:1 is only an approximation of swing.
- **Written:** write the counts for quarters, eighths, triplets, and sixteenths.
- **Practical:** keep quarters, eighths, triplets, and sixteenths steady against a metronome for at least one minute each.
- **Practical:** switch between straight and swung eighths on one chord without changing the tempo or the notes.
- **Aural:** hear whether a short example is straight or swung, and hear the backbeat.
- **Application:** play a chord progression from this unit in two feels with accurate time.
