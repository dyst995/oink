# Triads

**Prerequisites:** Intervals in music  
**Next:** Seventh chords

## Concept

A **triad** is three pitch classes stacked in thirds from a root: root, third, fifth. In degree formulas relative to a major scale on that root:

| Quality | Formula | Intervals from root |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Major | `1 3 5` | M3 + P5 (or M3 then m3 above that) |
| Minor | `1 ♭3 5` | m3 + P5 (or m3 then M3) |
| Diminished | `1 ♭3 ♭5` | m3 + d5 (two minor thirds) |
| Augmented | `1 3 ♯5` | M3 + A5 (two major thirds) |

The third decides major vs minor color. The fifth is perfect in major and minor; flatten it for diminished, raise it for augmented.

Spell with letters a third apart (skip one letter each time): from `C` → `E` → `G`. Then add accidentals so the intervals match the quality. `C E♭ G` is C minor — not `C D♯ G`, even if `D♯` and `E♭` are enharmonic. Chord spelling keeps the third-stack clear.

Common symbols: `C` or `Cmaj` (major), `Cm` or `Cmin` (minor), `Cdim` or `C°` (diminished), `Caug` or `C+` (augmented).

## Examples

- `G` major: `G B D` (`1 3 5`).
- `G` minor: `G B♭ D` (`1 ♭3 5`).
- `B` diminished: `B D F` (`1 ♭3 ♭5`) — the vii° triad in C major (preview).
- `C` augmented: `C E G♯` (`1 3 ♯5`).

## Exercises

1. Build and spell major, minor, and diminished triads on `D` and on `F`.
2. Name the quality: `A C E`, `E G♯ B`, `F♯ A C`, `A♭ C E♭`.
3. On the guitar, play a root on the A string, its major third and perfect fifth somewhere above it (any voicing). Then lower the third one fret for minor. Say the formula each time.

**Done when:** from any root you can spell all four triad qualities with correct letters and accidentals, and you can read `1 3 5` / `1 ♭3 5` as interval stacks.
