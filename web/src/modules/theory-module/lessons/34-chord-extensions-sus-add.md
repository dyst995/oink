# Suspensions, added tones, and extensions

**Prerequisites:** Seventh chords on guitar  
**Next:** Voice leading and guide tones

## Concept

Beyond triads and sevenths, symbols add or replace tones:

| Symbol family | Meaning | Example tones (C) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sus2` / `sus4` | 3rd replaced by 2nd or 4th | `Csus2` = `C D G`; `Csus4` = `C F G` |
| `add9` / `add2` | triad + 9th (2nd), **no 7th implied** | `Cadd9` = `C E G D` |
| `6` | triad + major 6th | `C6` = `C E G A` (often = `Am7` pitch set) |
| `m6` | minor triad + major 6th | `Cm6` = `C E♭ G A` |
| `9` | dominant 7 + 9th (7th implied) | `C9` = `C E G B♭ D` |
| `maj9` | maj7 + 9 | `Cmaj9` = `C E G B D` |
| `m9` | m7 + 9 | `Cm9` = `C E♭ G B♭ D` |
| `11` / `13` | further extensions; on guitar often omit 5 or even root | context-dependent |
| Altered (`b9`, `#9`, `#11`, `b13`) | chromatic tensions, usually on dominant | e.g. `G7♭9` |

**Critical distinctions:** `Cadd9` ≠ `C9` (no ♭7 in add9). `Csus2` has no third — quality is suspended, not major/minor until it resolves or context supplies one.

## Underlying logic

Extensions are scale degrees above the seventh chord (9 = 2, 11 = 4, 13 = 6), usually voiced above the core 3rd/7th. Guitar omits notes because six strings and playability limit density; keep **guide tones** (3 and 7) when the chord must speak, then add color tones.

Suspensions delay the third; they often resolve `sus4→3` or `sus2→3`.

## Musical significance

Pop ballads use `add9` and `sus`; funk/jazz use 9ths and alterations; singer-songwriter parts lean on `sus2`/`sus4` moving to triads. Reading charts correctly prevents playing a dominant 9 when the writer wanted a plain triad plus 9.

## Guitar application

On strings 4–3–2 (+ optional higher string):

- `C` = `10 9 8`
- `Csus2` ≈ replace `E` with `D` (e.g. adjust middle/top voices)
- `Cadd9` keep `E` and add `D` above
- Shell of `C9`: imply `E` + `B♭` (+ `D`) over a `C` bass elsewhere

Compare `Cadd9` vs `C9` by including or omitting `B♭`.

## Exercises

1. Spell `Gsus4`, `Gadd9`, `G9`, `Gmaj9`, `G6`. Circle the symbol that requires `F`.
2. Explain why `Am7` and `C6` can share pitch classes; how would bass/context decide the name?
3. On guitar, play `| D | Dsus2 | D | Dsus4 | D |` with clear resolutions back to the third.
4. Voice a `Cmaj7` and a `Cmaj9` (add `D`); then a `C7` and `C9` (add `D`, keep `B♭`).
5. For `G7♭9`, spell chord tones and play a shell + `A♭` color tone resolving into `C`.

## Mastery criteria

- **Written:** Distinguish sus/add/6/9/maj9/m9 spellings and which imply a 7th.
- **Conceptual:** Explain omitted notes on guitar vs full tertian stacks.
- **Practical:** Play sus and add9 motions resolving to triads; play a basic 9th color on a dominant.
