# Sevenths on guitar

**Prerequisites:** Seventh chords; triad string sets  
**Next:** Diatonic seventh harmony

## Concept

Guitar cannot always play all four notes of a seventh chord comfortably, and it often does not need to. A **shell voicing** is a stripped-down seventh chord: the **root**, the **third**, and the **seventh**. The third and seventh are the **guide tones**: they tell the listener whether the chord is major, minor, or dominant. The fifth is the most expendable chord tone because it rarely changes the chord's identity.

An **inversion** is a voicing whose lowest note is not the root. In first inversion the third is lowest, in second inversion the fifth, in third inversion the seventh. Slash chords name this: `Cmaj7/E` is `Cmaj7` with `E` in the bass.

You need three skills together: finding the root on a string, finding the third, and finding the seventh. Strings are numbered 6 (low E) to 1 (high E).

## Underlying logic

### Distances to memorise

From a root:

| Chord tone | Distance above root | Distance below the root's octave |
| --- | --- | --- |
| minor 3rd (`♭3`) | 3 semitones | 9 semitones |
| major 3rd (`3`) | 4 semitones | 8 semitones |
| minor 7th (`♭7`) | 10 semitones | a whole step below the octave |
| major 7th (`7`) | 11 semitones | a half step below the octave |

The octave of a note on string 6 is on string 4, two frets higher. The seventh is one or two frets below that octave: **go back one fret for maj7 or two frets for ♭7.** With a root on string 6 at fret `r`, the octave is on string 4 at fret `r+2`, so the ♭7 is on string 4 at fret `r` and the maj7 is on string 4 at fret `r+1`.

For the third, count open-string distances. Open string 3 is 15 semitones above open string 6 (an octave plus a minor third). So with a root on string 6 at fret `r`, the **minor** third is on string 3 at fret `r` and the **major** third is on string 3 at fret `r+1`.

For a root on string 5 at fret `r`, open string 3 is 10 semitones above open string 5, so the ♭7 is on string 3 at fret `r` and the maj7 is on string 3 at fret `r+1`. Open string 2 is 14 semitones above open string 5 (an octave plus a whole step), so the **minor** third is on string 2 at fret `r+1` and the **major** third is on string 2 at fret `r+2`.

These offsets work only for the string pairs stated. Other string pairs have different offsets because the `G` to `B` gap is a major third while the other gaps are fourths.

### Shells are movable

A shell has no open strings, so it slides up and down the neck: move it up one fret and every chord tone rises one half step, giving the same chord a half step higher. The only reliable facts are the chord tone relationships.

## Musical significance

Shells are the working vocabulary of rhythm guitar in jazz, funk, soul, and blues. They leave space for bass, keys, and melody. They also make harmonic motion obvious: when you move from `G7` to `Cmaj7` you can see and hear exactly which voices move.

The lowest note of a voicing is **not always the root.** `Am7` (`A C E G`) and `C6` (`C E G A`) contain the same four pitch classes. If the bass plays `A`, you hear `Am7`; if it plays `C`, you hear `C6`. The name comes from the context and the bass note, not the pitch classes alone.

## Guitar application

### Shells with the root on string 6

Frets are listed 6 to 1. `x` is muted.

| Chord | Frets | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Gmaj7` | `3x44xx` | G, F♯, B |
| `G7` | `3x34xx` | G, F, B |
| `Gm7` | `3x33xx` | G, F, B♭ |

The only changes are one fret on string 4 (7th) or one fret on string 3 (3rd). Fretting hand: use finger 1 on the root, finger 2 or 3 for the other notes, and let finger tips mute strings 5, 2, and 1.

### Shells with the root on string 5

| Chord | Frets | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Cmaj7` | `x3x45x` | C, B, E |
| `C7` | `x3x35x` | C, B♭, E |
| `Cm7` | `x3x34x` | C, B♭, E♭ |

### Full voicings that contain a shell

- `Fmaj7` `xx3210` is root position: `F A C E`.
- `Cmaj7` `x32000` is root position: `C E G B E`.
- Inversions: `Cmaj7/E` is `032000` (low `E` open on string 6 is the third in the bass). `Cmaj7/B` is `x22010` (`B` on string 5 fret 2 in the bass is the seventh, with `C` on string 2 fret 1 right above it).

### Connecting two chords

`G7` shell `3x34xx` to `Cmaj7` shell `x3x45x`. The `B` on string 3 fret 4 is the third of `G7` and the seventh of `Cmaj7`: it **stays put**. The `F` (string 4 fret 3) falls to `E` (string 2 fret 5), which is the third of `Cmaj7`. The root moves `G` to `C`. This is the tritone resolution from seventh-chord construction, on the neck.

## Exercises

1. Play the six shells above in order: `Gmaj7`, `G7`, `Gm7`, `Cmaj7`, `C7`, `Cm7`. Name the root, third, and seventh of each aloud while you play it.
2. Find the shell for `Bm7` with the root on string 6 (answer: root at fret 7, `7x77xx`: `B A D`). Check: the notes are `B` (string 6 fret 7), `A` (string 4 fret 7), `D` (string 3 fret 7).
3. Without a diagram, build `A7` with the root on string 6 (fret 5), then find the ♭7 on string 4 and the major third on string 3. Answer: `5x56xx` is `A G C♯`.
4. Play `G7` shell to `Cmaj7` shell. Say which note is common to both and on which string it sits.
5. Play only strings 4 to 1 as `2010` (notes `E G C E`). Add an open `A` (string 5) underneath: that is `Am7` (`x02010`). Now play the same four strings over a `C` on string 5 fret 3 (`x32010`): that is `C` major. Same upper notes, two different chords. Explain which note you hear as the root each time and why.

## Mastery criteria

- **Conceptual:** explain why the third and seventh carry the identity of a seventh chord, and why the fifth is usually safe to omit.
- **Written:** write the root, third, and seventh of `Dm7`, `G7`, `Cmaj7`, `F7`, `B♭maj7`.
- **Fretboard:** from any root on string 6 or 5, find the third and seventh for maj7, dominant 7, and m7 without a chart.
- **Fretboard:** play `G7` shell to `Cmaj7` shell and point to the held note.
- **Aural:** hear a shell voicing and say whether the chord is major 7, dominant 7, or minor 7.
- **Application:** explain why the same upper notes (`E G C E`) can be `Am7` or `C` major depending on the bass, and why `Am7` and `C6` share pitch classes without sharing a name.
