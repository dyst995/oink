# Arpeggios and chord-tone targeting

**Prerequisites:** Functional progressions; chord shapes as interval stacks  
**Next:** Pentatonic and blues as subsets

## Concept

An **arpeggio** is the chord tones played as a line: `1 3 5` or `1 3 5 7` in order (or broken order). Improvisation that “fits” a progression usually lands **chord tones on strong beats** for the chord of the moment, and uses scales/approaches between those targets.

Outline a progression by arpeggiating each symbol in time. That teaches the harmony to your ear and hands before you add decoration.

Targeting: toward a chord change, aim for the next chord’s root, third, or seventh (guide tones `3` and `7` are especially clear). The scale is the path; the chord tone is the destination.

## Examples

- Over `| C | G7 | C |`: arpeggiate C major, then G7 (`G B D F`), then C.
- Strong-beat targeting: on beat 1 of G7, land `B` or `F` (3 or ♭7); resolve into `C` or `E` when C returns.
- Same Cmaj7 arpeggio in two octaves — same tones, different register.

## Exercises

1. In `G` major, arpeggiate Imaj7 – V7 – Imaj7 slowly, one chord per bar, naming tones.
2. Improvise only on chord tones for `| Am7 | D7 | Gmaj7 |` (ii–V–I in G). No scale fillers yet.
3. Add approach notes: play any note a half step below each strong-beat target, then the target.

**Done when:** you can outline a short progression with arpeggios and consciously land chord tones on downbeats.
