# Guitar theory curriculum

Goal: musical independence on the guitar — understanding how music is organized, navigating the fretboard, hearing relationships, analyzing unfamiliar music, and improvising or composing with intent.

Audience: intermediate technical guitarists with theory gaps.

Principle: **theory, rhythm, ear, and guitar application develop together.** Reinforce earlier skills in later exercises.

---

## Stage 1 — Musical foundations

1. Pitch, octave, and pitch class  
2. The twelve pitch classes  
3. Half steps and whole steps  
4. Tuning and the open strings  
5. Locating pitch classes on the neck  
6. Octave relationships on the neck  
7. Pulse, meter, and rhythmic notation  
8. Subdivisions, syncopation, and feel  
9. Fretboard memorization (interactive practice)

## How lessons are taught

1. **Concept**  
2. **Underlying logic**  
3. **Musical significance**  
4. **Guitar application**  
5. **Exercises** (construct, play, hear, analyze)  
6. **Mastery criteria**  

Ear training uses guitar, voice, and external references. Self-played examples are not blind listening tests.

Rhythm from Stage 1 should reappear in scale, chord, and improvisation practice (assign a subdivision; keep a click).

---

## Deliberately later / optional depth

Full modulation schemas, advanced altered-dominant vocabularies, and dense upper-structure systems can expand after Stage 9–11 are solid.
